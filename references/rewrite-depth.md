# Rewrite Depth

## Default: balanced rewrite

Keep strong existing content. Rewrite the information that controls the recruiter’s decision. The aim is not a cosmetic keyword pass and not an unrecognizable new resume.

## Preserve

- Specific achievements, dates, titles, projects, data, and well-written bullets that already support the target role.
- User-protected template, photo, section, wording, length, and design constraints.
- Experiences that establish credibility, even if they are not the central target; compress or reorder rather than delete without reason.

## Rewrite decisively when needed

- The target job changes the core function, industry, metric, or stakeholder context.
- The existing resume is generic, task-only, outdated, badly ordered, or weak on outcomes.
- A recent or relevant experience should carry more weight than it currently does.

Prioritize, in order: target positioning, skills, recent or relevant experience, project bullets, and personal summary.

## Bullet construction

Use an evidence-based form where practical:

`action + method/tool + problem or operating context + result`

Do not force every bullet into the same pattern. A claim without a real result can still be useful if it makes the work concrete.

## Scale choices

- Light: retain layout and bullet sequence; sharpen selected verbs, methods, and JD terms.
- Balanced: reorder modules and bullets, rewrite central sections, retain strong facts and less relevant supporting sections.
- Deep: restructure the main narrative for a materially different role or industry while preserving all factual anchors and template constraints.

Choose the shallowest level that genuinely makes the resume competitive.
